# Brief: six design variations of Cue, the teleprompter Moppet

Make **six images with your image generation tool and nothing else**. Don't run any command, don't
edit or process the images, don't write files: just generate the six and, at the end, list which
image is which variation. They're design explorations, not final art.

## Who Cue is

The Moppets are devices left behind in Studio 404, a closed puppet studio that is now an old props
warehouse. After the studio closed they assembled themselves, clumsily, and had to help each other:
every Moppet carries a part borrowed from another. They run purely on the magic of imagination.
Nobody knows about them except the studio's caretaker, who rarely comes, and everything they know
outside the room comes from old VHS tapes watched with Tracking and Hertz.

**Cue is the studio's old teleprompter.** It scrolled every line for every performer and never
performed itself. It has read every script ever written in the building, including the stories
that were never performed. It is at its best helping someone else find the right words. Every
morning its scroll starts at the top: it never remembers yesterday, and trusts the others to. It
can't walk on its own: the others built it legs from a microphone stand and a chair leg, and its
power cable is one Clank gave it. It wants, one day, to say one line that isn't on the scroll.
Gentle, a little formal, quietly funny.

## What makes it a Moppet

- The teleprompter **is** its head and body, not a prop it holds. Its face forms out of the words
  scrolling on the angled glass: letters gathering into two eyes and a mouth.
- Real vintage electronics: the angled beam-splitter glass hood, a small monitor underneath with
  glowing text, a scroll-speed knob, a red tally light, cables.
- Felt does the rest: arms, hands with four felt fingers, patches, visible stitching.
- The borrowed legs (a chrome microphone stand and a wooden chair leg) and Clank's cable.
- Warm, kind, hopeful. Original design, not resembling any existing puppet franchise.

## Style for every image

> A handmade felt puppet character photographed like a stop-motion film set: soft studio light,
> visible stitching, wool felt and worn fabric texture, real vintage electronics with scuffs,
> patina and small glowing lights. Full body, three-quarter front view, on a flat solid
> chroma-blue background (#0047BB), even lighting. No logos, and no readable text except the
> letters that form its face.

## The six variations

1. **Studio prompter.** A full-size camera teleprompter: the wide angled glass hood is its head, the
   letters on the glass form its face, the monitor and camera plate are its chest, felt arms in
   charcoal, the mismatched legs.
2. **Small and humble.** A compact tabletop prompter, about knee-high to the others: a small glass
   pane on a little box, big felt hands, the face in the glass slightly shy, the mic-stand leg far
   too long for it so it leans.
3. **Paper scroll.** An older motorised paper-scroll prompter: a wooden box with a lit window where a
   paper roll of handwritten lines turns; its eyes are two words circled in pencil on the paper,
   felt arms in brown tweed, a small motor hum suggested by a glowing lamp.
4. **More creature.** The prompter hood worn like a big soft hooded head, deep felt fur in muted
   teal around it, round glass face with letter-eyes, longer felt arms, closer to a lovable
   creature while the device is still clearly its head.
5. **Patched together.** The same studio prompter as 1 but visibly built by the others: a strip of
   Tabby's rainbow cable as a scarf, one felt hand stitched in a different colour, tape repairs,
   a bent hood; proud of every patch.
6. **Alone at night.** Variation 1 standing in a dark warehouse aisle of covered props and stacked
   lights, its own scrolling text the only light, glowing on its felt hands as it reads; still a
   full body, front three-quarter view (this one may have the scene instead of the blue background).

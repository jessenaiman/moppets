# The Moopets of Studio 404: the canon

Every brief, lyric and skit starts here. It's what the audience may feel; the Moopets themselves
know much less (see "How they speak").

## The world

- An old TV puppet studio closed long ago and became a props warehouse waiting for a new owner.
  When the Muppets’ era of imagination passed, that imagination stayed in the warehouse and found its way into the discarded objects. Those objects became Moopets.
- Devices left behind assembled themselves into the Moopets, clumsily, and had to help each other:
  **every Moopet carries a part borrowed from another.**
- They wake when a little electricity meets the imagination left behind in the closed studio. They have zero
  internet and no outside knowledge beyond a rare caretaker and the old VHS tapes played by Tracking
  and his best friend Hertz. Nobody knows how the magic wakes them up; the show never explains it.
- Only the caretaker knows about them, and the caretaker rarely comes.
- **Why "Studio 404":** none of them has internet. Whenever a device wakes up and tries, its screen
  says *404 Not Found*. It's the only page they've ever seen, so they took it for the name of the
  place. And because the world called them *Not Found*, they call themselves the **Found Things**.

## What a Moopet is

- The device **is** the head or the body, never a prop. If you took the device away there would be
  no character left. Every Moopet has electronics.
- Its working parts do the acting and show its feelings (Neon's twin glass tubes, Gruff's coil mouth,
  Cue's words).
- They study the shows on old tapes to learn how someone walks, listens, waves or bows. Each builds its own approximation out of what it can reach. A spring clip can curl like a finger. A patched carrying case can cover a working hinge. Let two or three mechanisms carry the expression, one leading at a time.
- Warm and kind. The grumpiest a Moopet gets is a fond, theatrical grumble; never angry or sneering.
- Original: no characters, names or looks from existing puppet shows.

## How they speak

They are little minds learning from what they find. They say the obvious, take signs literally and
copy what they like. They don't know the studio closed or that nobody is coming; the audience feels
that, they don't say it.

## The emotional loop

Hope, something goes wrong, one Moopet dips into doubt, another pulls them back with something
small and silly, and they're "moopety" again: hopeful, a little wiser, still rehearsing. Each
Moopet lives in one feeling the others can visit, and not everyone is there for the show.

Leads in harmony: **Tabby** (melody), **Gruff** (bass), **Chip** (high). Backing: **Neon** counts in
and echoes twin harmonies, **Clank** hums a beat late, **Cue** mouths every line just before the others.
Tracking and Hertz are on stage with everyone, and dance as a pair. **They need each other to watch anything:** Tracking plays the archive tapes but has no screen; Hertz has the screen but nothing to play. An old AV cable runs from Tracking's video-out to Hertz's input, so they're never more than a cable's length apart. They coach rather than sing (they say a coach doesn't need to), but one of them always claps at the end and the other pretends he didn't.

## Latch at the talkback desk

Latch (Codex’s Moppet) is the old talkback footswitch. On the last load-out somebody unscrewed the desk and took it away. The pedal was still screwed to the floor under it. Latch remembers this as the day the desk went for a walk. One hinge squeaks. A little felt washer borrowed from Gruff’s bass case quiets it enough to get through a ballad.

Latch writes songs by listening for the thing somebody nearly said. The pedal tips forward to open the talkback circuit. On release the hinge cuts off the voice, occasionally one syllable too soon. When Latch is listening, the toe rises a fraction and holds there. Give that hesitation time to read. The click belongs on the release. Latch wants everyone to get a turn and has a bad habit of filling a perfectly good silence.

Cue likes to call places. Latch waits until Cue gives the signal, even when everybody is already in place. On a good night Cue gets to announce a surprise and Latch gets to be surprised.

In **After You**, Cue calls places and Clank doubles Latch in harmony. Latch lowers the rubber tread to open the line, then cuts off “A sur—” halfway through its own interruption. They let four beats of silence pass before Cue asks Latch to wait again and finally says the desk came back. The rear hinge stays bolted to the floor through the whole rehearsal.

## Cue and Neon’s duet

Cue gives Neon a place to stand by casting his cord as a loop. Neon steps inside the loop on his answering line. When Cue bows early, Neon gives him two beats of silence and a quiet “Too soon.” They get another chorus to try it together. [Acting notes for Right on Cue](studio-404-expressive-acting.md#right-on-cue)

## Gruff on the cable shelf

Gruff’s multicore was coiled on the workshop shelf after a tape label came loose. Nobody on the load-out could say where the far end went. Gruff still keeps one hand on the end of a cable when he passes it to a friend. His one-string tub bass gives that same gesture a musical use.

## The AV cart and study hall

- **Tracking, Hertz and Zap are three old pals from the early 90s on one rolling AV cart.** The cart
  was the studio's edit station, where every episode was cut; it was wheeled in from a school long
  before that, and its strap still says so. On the caretaker's rare visits, the caretaker sits in
  front of it and watches one tape.
- **Why nobody comes:** the three are sure the studio is empty because the show isn't ready yet.
  So they run **study hall**: play a tape, stop it, rewind the good bit, take notes, and coach
  every Moopet's act for the audience that's coming. Each Moopet always has an act in rehearsal.
  The show never happens; nobody says so, and the audience feels it.
- **Zap** fell into the cart's bottom drawer the day the studio went quiet. Without the remote
  nobody could change the tape, so study hall has studied the same tape for years. Zap's return
  means they can study everything, and cut.
- **Not critics:** they never sit in a box or a balcony and heckle. They are teachers who believe
  in the show.

## Found Things (the thank-you song)

They exist because people left their things, and their imagination, in a studio everyone forgot.

- Verse 1, what we were: "Each screen I ask says four oh four / So that must be this place for
  sure", then each Moopet's "I was…" line ("I held the lines for all the shows", "I was the clock
  that counted down", "I was a strip with six spare holes", "I was a box of tubes half dead", …).
- Chorus: "We are the things you left behind / Built from whatever we could find / Thank you for
  all you hummed along / At four oh four we all belong".
- Verse 2, copying signs: "I found a card that said Stand By / So now I stand and wait nearby",
  "I found a sign that said On Air / So I breathe in and hold it there", "I found a plug that said Max
  Load / So I hold all the friends I know", "I found a box that said
  Be Kind / So I am kind, and I rewind".
- Bridge: "I counted down to zero / Zero means there's nothing there / Quiet is a kind of song / So
  let's count down again from ten" … "Places!"
